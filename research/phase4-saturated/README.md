# Completed oversaturated stress study — not the primary load sweep

These results preserve the second Phase4 cohort: phase expiry was corrected, but the legacy corridor demand was still misconfigured. All30 matched trials and three additional240s safety trials completed without reported faults; no supervisor rejection occurred. The candidate failed the predeclared2% delay-reduction screen: -0.06695% reduction at scale0.75 (slight regression),+0.37398% at1.75. It was never qualified or enabled operationally.

The legacy rates were `exp(6)`, `exp(8)`, `exp(9)`, `exp(12)`, `exp(7)`, `exp(11)`:53 expected vehicles/second,190800/hour before scaling. SUMO interprets the exponential argument as a rate, not a mean headway. Both scales were consequently insertion-limited extreme overloads; they must not be described as normal versus heavy traffic. One fixed-time trial had122.53s mean departure delay among recorded trips, with additional unrecorded pending demand. Similar realized inflows across scales exposed the configuration error. [SUMO flow parameter documentation](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Definition_of_Vehicles%2C_Vehicle_Types%2C_and_Routes.html).

These weights are incompatible with the subsequent corrected-demand qualification fingerprint and remain disabled.

Data here remain useful as specifically labeled legacy oversaturation evidence, with completed-trip censoring and insertion-backlog limitations; they do not establish performance across a calibrated demand range or useful learned coordination.
